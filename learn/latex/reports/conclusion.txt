\chapter{CONCLUSION}

This study demonstrates that CF-MADRL delivers stronger traffic performance than traditional control in most tested cases. In the SUMO experiments, CF-MADRL reduced average waiting time versus fixed-time control by about 34\% at J1, 80\% at J2, and 51\% at J5, while showing comparable performance at J7. For queue length, it achieved large reductions at J2 (about 67\%) and moderate reductions at J5 (about 30\%) compared with fixed-time control. The cumulative cost metric shows the clearest advantage: CF-MADRL reduced cost by about 65\% at J1, 89\% at J2, 74\% at J5, and 43\% at J7. Overall, these results indicate that clustered federated multi-agent learning produces adaptive policies that outperform fixed-time baselines and remain competitive with Q-Table and DQN, especially at complex junctions.

The work contributes a full pipeline from simulation to a HIL prototype, where YOLO-based detection on Raspberry Pi drives real-time phase decisions via ESP32. Limitations include reliance on simulation, a fixed network topology, and a small detection dataset for the prototype. Future work should expand to real traffic networks, strengthen inter-junction awareness in hardware, scale to more junctions, and validate performance under sensor noise and communication delays. Potential applications include smart city traffic management, intelligent transportation systems, and controlled urban or campus road networks where privacy-preserving, adaptive signal control can reduce delays and congestion.
